[20506bef] (state: established; projection: lore_fact)
Roccid is from the noble House Drusus. Their standing amongst the other noble houses had remained high for centuries. They were known as merchants and traders, responsible for much of the goods imported from Phyro and Bastok.

Two years ago, the climate of the city started to change. The King decreed that Fleurite was a Kingdom alone — no more trade with outside nations would be permitted. House Drusus could have stood on its substantial wealth, but rumors started to circulate that they weren't just tolerant of outsiders — they actively supported them. Members of House Drusus were said to have even married outsider peoples. It wasn't long before House Drusus was stripped of its nobility.

In an attempt to get back into the graces of the Kingdom, Roccid's parents sent him to the castle to train with the Assassins Guild. There he was indoctrinated into believing the many evils of outsiders and the wrongdoings of the past peoples of Fleurite. One such individual was Ruhrogue — a beloved disciple of the master Ruh, who viciously betrayed and murdered his master, then fled the city to live amongst the outsiders he was supporting.

Roccid was eventually sent to assassinate Ruhrogue. He failed and was mortally wounded by Ruhrogue in the encounter. Penelope, a member of Ruhrogue's party, found Roccid dying in an alley and healed him. Since then, Roccid sought out Ruhrogue and became his apprentice. He has grown disillusioned by Fleurite's current state and sees Ruhrogue as a possible solution to the corruption.

Roccid spent several days alone at the Monastery with Sorin while the party ascended Ishi'ra'la. They did not know each other much before, but have grown close. Both raised on stories of their people's good deeds, both disillusioned by Fleurite. They made a pact to support Ruhrogue in any attempt to repair the corruption in Fleurite.
(sources: npcs/Roccid.md)

[55d166e3] (state: established; projection: lore_fact)
Born in Karinth village, a small settlement in the wilds to the south. His village was spared the brutality of the fighting, but the devastation left the area unstable. His family moved to Fleurite so his father could find more reliable work as a carpenter. They gained some level of comfort over the years in Fleurite.

Sorin's father Carylle took him to see King Peter's speeches on many occasions, and Sorin became enamored with the elegance and humanity of the King.

Last year, when he came of age, the King's men appeared at his house and told his father that Sorin was required to appear at the castle for conscription service. Sorin felt this was a good thing — he could do his part to protect the Kingdom for the fair and noble King. He had been hearing of horrific acts in the region caused by the King, but he always defended his King to anyone.

The first few months of conscription were hard, but Sorin believed it was just a training style for his commanders to be cruel. His aptitude for the bow led him to be put with the Outriders. He followed Inquisitor Eustice's direction at every village they came across. Being told of the heresy of the village and their people's hatred of the King, Sorin believed they were doing the right thing.

All until the fall of Veram. Veram was mostly women and children, and the Inquisitor slaughtered them all. If the party had not found him at Veram, Sorin was planning on fleeing the city, though he feared retribution against his family if he did. They must have been told of his death by now.

Sorin spent several days alone at the Monastery with Roccid while the party ascended Ishi'ra'la. They did not know each other much before, but have grown close. Both raised on stories of their people's good deeds, both disillusioned by Fleurite's current state. They made a pact to support Ruhrogue in any attempt to repair the corruption in Fleurite.
(sources: npcs/sorin-tarkohv.md)

1. Story Time — The Descent:

As the party walks the mountain trail, Sorin, Roccid, and Dariferra each share stories. This section is ambiance — the last moment of relative peace before the crisis hits. The GM can draw from the NPCs' backgrounds to prompt conversation.

Ambiance — intersperse between stories:

The trail winds through patches of wild mint and thyme. Every step crushes a little more fragrance into the air — sweet, green, alive.

A hawk circles lazily overhead, riding a thermal. It doesn't seem to have a care in the world. Neither does the marmot that watches you from a rock, then vanishes with a whistle.

The wind carries the sound of a distant waterfall — steady, soft, like the mountain breathing.

A cluster of blue flowers grows from a crack in the stone, swaying. You can't name them. They don't seem to need you to.

Somewhere below, a bird calls — three notes, then silence. Then three notes again. Another answers from the other side of the valley.

The light filters through the pines in long, dusty beams. For a moment, the whole world feels slow and golden.

Sorin — Growing up in Fleurite, the King's speeches

If prompted, Sorin tells his story in his own words. Read or paraphrase:

"I was born in Karinth. Little village south of here. My da — Carylle — he's a carpenter. We moved to Fleurite when I was small because the wilds went bad after the fighting. Too many wolves, too many desperate people. Da said Fleurite had walls and work. He was right about the work."

"He built things. Cabinets, shutters, a roof for the tannery. Good hands. Honest work. We weren't rich, but we had enough. Ma kept a garden. I had a spot by the window where I'd carve little birds out of scrap wood. It was a good life."

"Da took me to see the King speak. First time, I was maybe ten. King Peter stood on the balcony and talked about building a kingdom where people didn't have to be afraid. His voice carried — not loud, but like he was talking to each person alone. I believed him. I believed everything he said."

"We went every time after that. I'd stand on my toes to see over the crowd. Da would lift me up on his shoulders when I was little. The King was... elegant. Kind. He seemed like he cared. I thought that's what a king should be."

"Last year they came for me. The King's men. They told Da I had to report for conscription. I didn't want to leave, but I thought — this is my duty. This is how I serve the King who gave us a home. I told Da it would be fine. I told Ma I'd be back before they knew it."

"I was wrong."

"The training was hard. They broke men who couldn't keep up. I thought it was just the way of things — make you hard so you don't break when it matters. But it wasn't hardening. It was cruelty. I see that now."

"I was good with a bow. That's the only reason I survived. They put me with the Outriders. Under Inquisitor Eustice. He told us we were rooting out heresy. That every village we visited had turned against the King. That they hated us. That they would kill us if we didn't kill them first."

"I believed him. I wanted to believe him. Because if I didn't, then what was I doing there?"

"Then Veram."

"Veram was mostly women and children. There was no fighting. There was no resistance. Eustice gave the order and they just... died. I didn't draw my bow. I stood there and watched. I couldn't stop it. I couldn't even move."

"I wanted to run. I had my hand on my pack before the bodies were cold. But if I ran, they'd go after my family. Da. Ma. They'd say I was a traitor, and they'd hurt the people I love to make an example. So I stayed. I stayed and I followed and I hated every step."

"If you hadn't found me at Veram, I would have run anyway. Somewhere. Anywhere. I don't know if I would have made it. But I couldn't stay. Not after that."

"They probably think I'm dead now. Told Da that his son died serving the King. Maybe they even gave him a medal. I just need to get word to him. Let him know I'm alive. Let him know I'm sorry."

Roccid — House Drusus, the Assassins Guild, and Ruhrogue

If prompted, Roccid tells his story in his own words. Read or paraphrase:

"You want to know how I ended up following a man I was sent to kill?"

"I was born into House Drusus. You've probably never heard of us — not anymore. But once, we were the biggest merchants in Fleurite. Everything that came in from Phyro and Bastok? That was us. Silks, spices, steel. My family built half the warehouses on the east side."

"Then the King decided Fleurite didn't need the outside world anymore. No more trade. No more foreign goods. We could have weathered it — we had coin enough. But the rumors started. House Drusus doesn't just tolerate outsiders. House Drusus supports them. House Drusus marries them. Next thing you know, we're stripped. Titles gone. Lands seized. Name ruined."

"My parents panicked. They needed to get back in the King's good graces, and they had only one thing left to offer — me. So they sent me to the castle. To the Assassins Guild."

"They told me the Guild would make me useful. And they did. They filled my head with everything the King wanted me to believe. That outsiders are corrupt. That the old houses who traded with them were traitors. That the worst of them was a man named Ruhrogue — a beloved disciple who murdered his own master, Master Ruh, and fled to live with the very outsiders he'd been supporting."

"I believed it. I was ready to kill him. I trained for months, and when they gave me the mark, I went."

"I failed. He beat me. I was bleeding out in an alley, and I thought — this is it. This is how a Drusus dies. A disposable son, sent to die for a family that already lost everything."

"But I didn't die. Penelope found me. Healed me. And when I woke up, I sought Ruhrogue out again. For a week I studied him and his friends. I learned who he was. Not the monster I'd been told about. Just a man. A man doing his part to save the world."

"He taught me everything after that. Not assassin work — real things. How the world works. What loyalty actually means. And somewhere along the way, I stopped being the son my parents sent to die and started being someone else."

"I don't know if my family is still alive. I don't know if they'd even want to see me. But I know this — everything their Kingdom told me about Ruhrogue was a lie. And if they lied about him, they lied about everything."

Dariferra — Before the awakening, what he's losing

Dariferra talks about who he was before the psychic link to Romulus. A person with ordinary memories, ordinary wants. The link is taking pieces of him. He may share a memory he's afraid of losing — a face, a place, a feeling.

GM guidance: Let the players guide the conversation. Offer a prompt for each NPC if the party engages, but don't force all three. The goal is texture — connection before the storm.

2. The Warning:

Dariferra stops mid-stride, or slows. The psychic leak from Romulus sharpens — not words but emotional tone: smug satisfaction, vindictive pleasure, a sense of moves being made.

Read-aloud:

Dariferra stops walking. He's staring at nothing — or rather, through it.

"Something's wrong," he says slowly. "The future is changing. I can feel it — like a current shifting underground. Romulus is moving pieces. Multiple pieces, all at once."

"He's pleased with himself. That's the worst part. He thinks he's winning."

3. The Overlook:

The party continues until the trail opens onto a broad stone shelf — the last high vantage before the switchbacks drop into the foothills. The full valley of Illisan spreads below.

Read-aloud:

The trail widens and the trees fall away. You step onto a stone shelf that overlooks the entire valley — the last high view before the road descends into the foothills.

Below, the valley of Illisan stretches like a map: the walled city of Fleurite to the southwest, the dark line of the central river, the patchwork of farms and estates between.

But three things pull your eye immediately — three wrong things in the landscape.

To the east, over the sea, a massive winged silhouette flies against the clouds. Too large to be a bird. Too purposeful to be a cloud formation. It's heading out over the ocean, toward the volcanic island on the horizon.

To the southwest, smoke rises from Fleurite — not the ordinary smoke of a hundred hearths, but thick black columns in the outer districts. Something is burning.

And below, too far to make out details but unmistakable — dust and movement along the roads leading toward the exile camp. Columns of soldiers, marching.

4. The Assessment:

The party discusses. Provide natural information from what they see:

Ishi'go'dan: That wing shape, that scale — there's only one thing it can be. He's flying east.
Fleurite smoke: Multiple columns, outer districts. Not an accident. This is a purge.
Troop movement: Heading toward where the exile camp is. Not a random patrol — purposeful columns.

Dariferra may get additional fragments from Romulus's surface mind:

"He's not just doing one thing. He's doing all of it. He wants to hurt everyone at once."

If pressed, Dariferra can sense:
Ishi'go'dan was commanded east — ordered to destroy something.
The smoke from Fleurite is punishment — vindictive, not strategic.
The troops are preemptive — Romulus doesn't know exactly where the camp is, but he knows the general area and is sending forces to sweep.

He cannot sense:
Exact destination, timeframes, or strategic detail
That Ishi'go'dan is heading to Tsunadis
That Ragga'na'ken is being summoned
The camp's internal divisions

5. The Decision:

The party must decide where to go. This is the climax of the scene — everything before it has led here. Reinforce that they can only respond to one — by the time they deal with one, the others will have resolved.

Possible questions from Sorin/Roccid:

"How far to the camp from here?"
"What could we even do against something that size?"
"Are there people in those burning districts?"
"How long before those troops reach the camp?"

Answers (from general knowledge):
Camp: ~half a day's forced march, slightly longer normal pace
Fleurite: ~a day's travel
Ishi'go'dan: already airborne, heading away — they'd need a ship or flight to intercept
